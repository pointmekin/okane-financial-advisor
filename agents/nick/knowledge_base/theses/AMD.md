# AMD -- Investment Thesis

## Original Thesis

AMD is the credible number-two in AI accelerators, positioned to capture meaningful share as hyperscalers diversify beyond NVIDIA's dominant GPU platform. The OpenAI partnership (6GW deployment, 160M share warrant at $0.01) and Meta partnership (estimated $60B over multiple years, 6GW deployment) together represent up to 12GW of committed AMD Instinct GPU capacity -- an unprecedented demand foundation that validates AMD's AI accelerator competitiveness. Data Center revenue hit a record $5.775B in Q1 2026 (57% YoY growth, now 56% of total revenue) and is on a $23B+ annual run rate. The MI450 next-generation GPU is sampling to customers with demand forecasts exceeding internal expectations. AMD also holds a strong position in server CPUs (EPYC with 25%+ share) and consumer processors (Ryzen), providing diversified revenue that reduces dependence on any single product cycle. A net cash position of ~$9.1B ($12.3B cash vs. $3.2B debt) provides financial flexibility. The expanding gross margin (53% GAAP in Q1 2026, up from 50% a year ago) and record quarterly FCF of $2.566B demonstrate operating leverage that should accelerate as Data Center scales further.

## Why Now (Opportunity)

Two transformative catalysts have emerged simultaneously. First, the OpenAI and Meta partnerships collectively represent the largest AI infrastructure commitments by any single chipmaker outside of NVIDIA, providing multi-year revenue visibility in the tens of billions. Second, the MI450 GPU -- now sampling to customers -- has demand forecasts exceeding AMD's initial expectations, suggesting the product pipeline is robust through H2 2026 and beyond. These partnerships also address the historical weakness of AMD's ROCm software ecosystem: Meta's co-development commitment on ROCm brings significant engineering resources to mature the platform. The Embedded segment (9% of revenue) showing its first positive growth in several quarters (6% YoY) suggests the cyclical downturn is bottoming, providing an additional tailwind.

## Key Business Metrics to Track

- **Data Center segment revenue growth rate:** Q1 2026 was +57% YoY ($5.775B); directional expectation is sustained above 40% as MI350 scales and MI450 launches in H2 2026
- **GAAP gross margin trajectory:** Q1 2026 was 53% (up from 50% YoY); directional expectation is continued expansion toward 55-56% as Data Center mix increases, with Non-GAAP guidance for Q2 at ~56%
- **ROCm software ecosystem maturity:** The Meta co-development partnership is a key catalyst; watch for evidence of AMD GPUs winning AI training workloads (not just inference) at hyperscalers
- **MI450 launch execution and customer adoption:** Currently sampling; customer forecasts exceed expectations. Any delay or loss of a major commitment would be a material negative signal

## Kill Conditions

> Kill Conditions are specific, fundamental, and measurable. They are NOT price targets or drawdown thresholds. Triggering one requires immediate review and likely exit.

1. **[KC-1] Data Center segment revenue growth decelerates below 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters.** Data Center is the core growth engine, currently growing at 57% YoY. A deceleration below 20% would indicate that either NVIDIA's competitive moat is structurally insurmountable, hyperscaler custom silicon is displacing AMD GPUs, or the AI infrastructure build-out is slowing -- any of which would break the thesis that AMD is a credible AI accelerator alternative at scale.

2. **[KC-2] GAAP gross margin falls below 48% for two consecutive quarters.** The current 53% gross margin reflects favorable mix shift toward high-margin Data Center products. A decline below 48% would indicate either aggressive pricing pressure from NVIDIA, product mix regression toward lower-margin segments, or manufacturing cost issues -- all of which would undermine the operating leverage thesis.

3. **[KC-3] OpenAI or Meta publicly reduces or cancels its AMD Instinct GPU commitment.** The two partnerships are the foundation of AMD's AI accelerator credibility. If either customer publicly scales back its AMD deployment in favor of NVIDIA or custom silicon, it would signal that AMD's AI accelerator competitiveness is not yet sufficient for the largest workloads, invalidating the demand visibility thesis.
