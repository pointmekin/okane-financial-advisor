# NVDA -- Investment Thesis

## Original Thesis

NVIDIA owns the dominant full-stack platform for AI compute. The CUDA software ecosystem has created a deep developer moat with 5M+ programmers, making NVIDIA GPUs the default for AI training and inference workloads. This is not merely a hardware advantage -- it is an ecosystem lock-in analogous to Intel's x86 dominance in the PC era, but with a far larger total addressable market. Data Center revenue of $193.7B in FY2026 (growing 68% YoY and representing ~90% of total revenue) demonstrates that hyperscaler demand for AI infrastructure remains in an early-cycle build-out phase. The Groq acquisition strengthens positioning in inference, and the Rubin next-generation platform provides a credible product roadmap extending into FY2028. The company generates extraordinary profitability: $120.1B net income, $60.9B FCF, and ~60% operating margin in FY2026, all while investing $41.8B in CapEx and returning $41.4B to shareholders.

## Why Now (Opportunity)

Three converging factors make this a compelling entry. First, the Q1 FY2027 revenue guide of ~$78B implies continued sequential acceleration, signaling that AI infrastructure demand is not plateauing despite investor fears. Second, the Groq acquisition positions NVIDIA more aggressively in AI inference, addressing the competitive threat from custom inference chips before it materializes. Third, the gross margin compression to 71.1% (from 75.0%) is largely driven by one-time H20 export control charges and Blackwell transition costs -- not structural pricing pressure. If margins recover toward the mid-70s as Blackwell Ultra ramps and one-time charges fade, operating leverage could drive meaningful earnings upside. The combined hyperscaler CapEx estimate of ~$700B for 2026 provides demand visibility extending multiple quarters.

## Key Business Metrics to Track

- **Data Center revenue growth rate:** FY2026 was +68% YoY ($193.7B); directional expectation is deceleration but sustained above 30% as AI infrastructure build-out continues through the Blackwell Ultra and Rubin cycles
- **Gross margin trajectory:** FY2026 was 71.1% (compressed from 75.0% by $4.5B H20 charge and Blackwell transition); directional expectation is recovery toward 73-75% as one-time charges fade and Blackwell Ultra yields improve
- **Customer concentration risk:** Four customers each >10% of revenue (22%, 15%, 13%, 11% in Q3 FY2026); watch for whether hyperscaler custom silicon (Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium) begins displacing NVIDIA share at any single customer
- **Free Cash Flow growth relative to CapEx:** FY2026 FCF was $60.9B (+25%) while CapEx was $41.8B (+350%); CapEx is accelerating faster than FCF, which is sustainable only if Data Center demand persists

## Kill Conditions

> Kill Conditions are specific, fundamental, and measurable. They are NOT price targets or drawdown thresholds. Triggering one requires immediate review and likely exit.

1. **[KC-1] Gross margin falls below 65% for two consecutive quarters absent one-time charges.** FY2026 gross margin of 71.1% already absorbs the H20 charge and Blackwell transition costs. A structural decline below 65% on an adjusted basis would indicate that hyperscaler custom silicon or AMD competition is eroding NVIDIA's pricing power -- the core of the moat thesis.

2. **[KC-2] Data Center revenue declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters.** Data Center is 90% of revenue and the sole growth engine. A sustained sequential decline would signal that the AI infrastructure build-out has reached a demand cliff -- the central bear case -- and that the CapEx cycle has peaked. This would be fundamentally different from quarterly variability; two consecutive quarters of decline would confirm a trend.

3. **[KC-3] NVIDIA loses a hyperscaler customer representing >10% of revenue to a custom silicon or AMD alternative.** The customer concentration risk is material. If any of the four customers exceeding 10% of revenue publicly announces a shift away from NVIDIA GPUs for AI training workloads, it would validate the bear case that hyperscalers can build or buy adequate alternatives, breaking the ecosystem lock-in thesis.
