## Business Overview

NVIDIA designs GPU-accelerated computing platforms for gaming, data centers, automotive, and AI. The company operates through two reportable segments:

- **Compute & Networking** ($193.5B revenue): Data center GPUs (Hopper H100/H200, Blackwell B200/B300), networking (InfiniBand, Ethernet), AI inference and training platforms, DGX systems, and automotive AI processors.
- **Graphics** ($22.5B revenue): GeForce GPUs for gaming, RTX professional visualization products, and OEM solutions.

Key end markets: Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, Automotive, OEM & Other.

NVIDIA employs approximately 42,000 people, with 31,000 in R&D. The US accounts for 69% of revenue; international markets 31%.

**Strategic highlights:**
- Blackwell architecture began volume shipments in FY2026
- Groq acquisition via $17B license deal ($14.4B goodwill) to expand AI inference capabilities
- Rubin next-generation platform announced for H2 FY2027
- Subject to US export controls effectively foreclosing the China market (H20/H200 restrictions resulted in a $4.5B charge)

## Risk Factors (Top 5)

1. **China Export Controls / Effective Market Foreclosure**: US government restrictions on H20, H200, and other products effectively foreclose the China market. The $4.5B charge reflects stranded inventory. Further restrictions could materialize.

2. **Supply/Demand Mismatches**: Unprecedented demand for AI training and inference hardware has created long lead times. Manufacturing commitments total $50.3B and cloud service commitments total $26B. Any disruption in supply could materially impact revenue.

3. **Competition**: AMD (MI300X/MI400), Intel (Gaudi), custom ASICs from Google (TPUs), Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia), and other cloud companies building in-house silicon threaten NVIDIA's pricing power and market share.

4. **Dependency on TSMC and Third-Party Suppliers**: NVIDIA is heavily reliant on TSMC for advanced node fabrication (4nm, 3nm). Any disruption at TSMC or concentration risk in Taiwan could severely impact production.

5. **Regulatory Scrutiny**: Increasing antitrust scrutiny globally, evolving AI regulation (EU AI Act, potential US AI regulation), and national security review of large investments (e.g., $5B Intel investment subject to regulatory approval).

## Competitive Position

**Strengths:**
- Dominant market share in AI training GPUs (>80% estimated)
- CUDA software ecosystem creates deep developer lock-in with 5M+ developers
- Full-stack offering: GPUs, networking (InfiniBand/Mellanox), software (CUDA, TensorRT, Triton)
- Blackwell architecture provides significant performance/watt improvements
- Groq acquisition strengthens inference positioning

**Weaknesses/Threats:**
- Hyperscalers increasingly building custom silicon (Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium)
- Export controls remove China as a growth market
- Gross margin pressure from product transitions and competitive pricing
- Customer concentration: Four direct customers each >10% of revenue (22%, 15%, 13%, 11%)
