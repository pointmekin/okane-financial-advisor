## Business Overview

Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a global semiconductor company offering high-performance computing and visualization products. AMD designs and integrates its products across CPU, GPU, FPGA, adaptive System-on-Chip (SoC), and DSP technologies.

**Reportable Segments (reorganized in FY2025):**
1. **Data Center** -- Server CPUs (EPYC), data center GPUs (Instinct MI300/MI350 series), DPUs (Pensando), and related software (ROCm)
2. **Client and Gaming** -- Desktop and notebook processors (Ryzen), discrete GPUs (Radeon), and semi-custom SoC products for gaming consoles
3. **Embedded** -- Embedded processors, FPGAs, and adaptive SoCs (Xilinx acquisition)

**Key Strategic Initiatives:**
- AI infrastructure buildout with AMD Instinct GPU accelerators
- ZT Systems acquisition ($4.4B) for rack-scale AI infrastructure; manufacturing business subsequently divested to Sanmina
- OpenAI partnership: AMD to supply GPUs for up to 6GW deployment; OpenAI received warrant for 160M AMD shares at $0.01/share
- Helios AI rack-scale platform development
- ROCm open-source software stack for AI/HPC workloads

**Employees:** Approximately 28,000 as of December 27, 2025

**Data Center Drivers:** Strong growth in AMD Instinct GPU shipments (MI300X, MI300A), EPYC server CPU adoption (4th and 5th Gen), and hyperscaler deployments.

**Embedded Decline:** Continued inventory correction and soft demand in industrial, automotive, and communications markets.

## Risk Factors (Top 5)

1. **Export Controls and Geopolitical Risk:** U.S. government restrictions on semiconductor exports to China and other jurisdictions. AMD recorded an $800M charge related to MI308 export restrictions (later partially reversed by $360M). Further restrictions could materially impact revenue and operations.

2. **Intense Competition and Technology Cycles:** AMD faces competition from NVIDIA (dominant in data center GPUs), Intel (CPUs), Qualcomm, Apple, and others. Failure to maintain technological leadership or execute on product roadmaps could result in market share loss.

3. **Customer Concentration:** A significant portion of revenue comes from a limited number of large customers (hyperscalers, console OEMs). Loss of a major customer or reduction in orders would materially affect results.

4. **Manufacturing Dependence on Third-Party Foundries:** AMD is fabless and relies on TSMC for manufacturing. Supply disruptions, capacity constraints, or foundry pricing changes could materially impact product availability and margins.

5. **Acquisition Integration and Execution Risk:** Recent large acquisitions (Xilinx, ZT Systems, Pensando) require successful integration. Failure to realize expected synergies or retention of key talent could impair long-term value.

## Competitive Position

**Strengths:**
- #2 in data center GPUs with AMD Instinct platform; gaining share against NVIDIA
- EPYC server CPU market share gains vs. Intel (estimated 25%+ server CPU share)
- Strong consumer brand with Ryzen processors competing effectively vs. Intel Core
- Growing AI software ecosystem (ROCm) and partnerships with major hyperscalers
- Diversified revenue across data center, client, gaming, and embedded markets
- Net cash position ($10.6B cash vs. $3.3B debt) providing financial flexibility

**Weaknesses:**
- Data center GPU software ecosystem (ROCm) still lags NVIDIA CUDA significantly
- Embedded segment facing cyclical downturn
- Gaming console revenue dependent on Sony/Microsoft product cycles (declining phase)
- Behind NVIDIA in AI training market share

**Key Product Roadmap:**
- MI350 series (MI355X) -- next-generation data center GPU
- MI450/MI455X -- announced, sampling to customers
- MI500 series -- future generation
- 6th Gen EPYC "Venice"/"Verano" -- upcoming server CPU
- Helios rack-scale AI platform
