# BRK.B -- Investment Thesis

## Original Thesis

Berkshire Hathaway is the ultimate permanent capital compounder -- a diversified collection of high-quality operating businesses (insurance, railroad, energy, manufacturing, retail) funded by the lowest-cost capital structure in corporate history. Insurance float of $176.9B generates massive investment income at near-zero cost, while operating earnings of $44.49B in FY2025 and $11.35B in Q1 2026 (+18% YoY) demonstrate that the underlying businesses produce substantial, growing cash flows regardless of market conditions. The fortress balance sheet -- $397B in cash and U.S. Treasury Bills as of Q1 2026 -- provides unmatched financial strength and the optionality to deploy capital into distressed opportunities during market dislocations. This is not a thesis about rapid growth; it is a thesis about downside protection, disciplined capital allocation, and compounding over decades. The leadership transition from Warren Buffett to Greg Abel on January 1, 2026 appears to be executing smoothly, with Buffett endorsing Abel's first quarter performance with a "100% score" and Abel reaffirming the conglomerate structure and operational excellence focus.

## Why Now (Opportunity)

Two factors converge. First, the leadership transition provides early evidence of continuity: Q1 2026 operating earnings of $11.35B (+18% YoY) under Greg Abel's first quarter as CEO exceeded expectations, and Abel's commitment to the conglomerate structure and operational excellence (including targeted adoption of narrow AI at BNSF and BHE) provides a credible path to margin improvement at underperforming subsidiaries. Second, Berkshire's record $397B cash position -- accumulated through 14 consecutive quarters of net equity selling -- positions the company as the buyer of choice during the next market dislocation. Buffett's characterization of the current investing environment as "not ideal" is a contrarian signal: when the environment inevitably becomes ideal (for distressed buyers), Berkshire will have both the capital and the willingness to deploy at prices that generate outsized returns.

## Key Business Metrics to Track

- **Operating earnings growth:** FY2025 was $44.49B (-6% YoY due to insurance catastrophe losses); Q1 2026 was $11.35B (+18% YoY). Directional expectation is mid-single-digit growth driven by BNSF operational improvements, BHE rate case recoveries, and insurance investment income from the T-bill portfolio
- **Insurance underwriting profitability:** Q4 2025 was impacted by catastrophe losses and GEICO reserve adjustments; GEICO earnings dropped 34% YoY in Q1 2026. Watch for whether underwriting margins recover or whether competitive pressure in auto insurance is structural
- **Cash and T-bill position relative to total assets:** Currently $397B in liquidity on $1.25T in total assets (~32%). This ratio will decline when Berkshire deploys capital into acquisitions; the pace of deployment signals management's view of opportunity attractiveness
- **Book value per Class B share growth:** ~$473 at year-end 2025, up ~12% YoY. This is the most important long-term metric for Berkshire -- it measures the compounding of intrinsic value

## Kill Conditions

> Kill Conditions are specific, fundamental, and measurable. They are NOT price targets or drawdown thresholds. Triggering one requires immediate review and likely exit.

1. **[KC-1] Operating earnings decline for two consecutive years on a full-year basis.** Berkshire's operating earnings have historically grown over time due to the compounding nature of its businesses and the interest income from its cash pile. Two consecutive years of decline would indicate either structural deterioration in the operating businesses (beyond normal insurance cyclicality) or that the massive cash pile is earning inadequate returns relative to the opportunity cost of not deploying it into productive assets.

2. **[KC-2] Berkshire initiates a large-scale acquisition (>$50B) outside its core competencies of insurance, railroads, energy, manufacturing, and retail.** The thesis rests on disciplined capital allocation. A transformative acquisition in an unfamiliar sector (e.g., technology, crypto, speculative growth) would signal that the Buffett/Abel discipline has eroded and that the cash pile is being deployed for ego or FOMO rather than value.

3. **[KC-3] PacifiCorp wildfire liabilities exceed $10B in cumulative accruals, or Berkshire breaks up the conglomerate structure.** PacifiCorp wildfire liabilities are currently ~$2.9B accrued. A ten-fold increase would materially impair BHE's balance sheet and signal unquantifiable tail risk. Similarly, a decision to break up the conglomerate would fundamentally alter the permanent capital structure that is central to the thesis.
