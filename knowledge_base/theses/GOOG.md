# GOOG -- Investment Thesis

## Original Thesis

Alphabet owns the world's most valuable attention graph. Google Search processes trillions of queries annually and monetizes them through an advertising system that has been refined for over two decades, creating a feedback loop of data, relevance, and revenue that no competitor has replicated. This is not a technology moat alone -- it is a behavioral moat: users default to Google because it works, and advertisers follow the users. The company is now extending this dominance into AI through Gemini (already embedded across all 15 half-billion-user products) and Google Cloud, which grew revenue 63% YoY in Q1 2026 to a $20B quarterly run-rate. Cloud operating income more than doubled in FY2025 to $13.9B, proving the segment has crossed the profitability inflection point. The combination of a monopolistic search franchise (73% of total revenue), an accelerating cloud platform, and a fortress balance sheet ($126.8B in cash and marketable securities against $46.5B in long-term debt as of Dec 2025) makes Alphabet a compounding machine with multiple growth engines.

## Why Now (Opportunity)

Two inflection points are converging simultaneously. First, Google Cloud has reached scale profitability -- operating income went from $6.1B to $13.9B in a single year (FY2025), and Q1 2026 revenue of $20.0B suggests a $80B+ annual run-rate with accelerating growth (+63% YoY). Second, AI integration across the product suite (AI Overviews, AI Mode in Search, Gemini Enterprise) is expanding the monetization surface of Search without cannibalizing core query volume -- paid clicks grew 6% while cost-per-click grew 7% in FY2025, indicating healthy expansion rather than dilution. The revenue backlog of $467.6B as of Q1 2026 (mostly Cloud) provides unusual forward visibility for a company at this scale.

## Key Business Metrics to Track

- **Google Cloud revenue growth rate:** 63% YoY in Q1 2026; directional expectation is deceleration from this extraordinary pace but sustained above 30% as AI infrastructure demand persists
- **Google Services operating margin:** FY2025 was ~40.7% ($139.4B / $342.7B); watch for compression from rising content costs and legal settlements
- **CapEx as a percentage of revenue:** FY2025 was 22.7% ($91.4B / $402.8B), expected to rise further in FY2026; this is the key variable determining whether free cash flow can grow alongside operating income
- **Consolidated operating margin:** 32% in both FY2024 and FY2025; sustaining this despite massive CapEx increases and lower-margin Cloud mix shift would confirm operating leverage is intact

## Kill Conditions

> Kill Conditions are specific, fundamental, and measurable. They are NOT price targets or drawdown thresholds. Triggering one requires immediate review and likely exit.

1. **[KC-1] Consolidated operating margin falls below 25% for two consecutive quarters.** This would indicate that the massive CapEx build-out ($91.4B in FY2025, accelerating) is not generating adequate returns, or that competitive pressure on Search and Cloud pricing is structural. The 32% operating margin in FY2025 already absorbs enormous investment; compression below 25% signals the investment thesis on returns on capital is broken.

2. **[KC-2] Google Cloud revenue growth decelerates below 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters while operating margin in that segment falls below 10%.** Cloud is the primary growth vector justifying the current level of CapEx investment. If Cloud growth slows to below 20% AND profitability compresses, it would mean the AI infrastructure build-out is producing commoditized capacity with no pricing power -- a structural problem, not a cyclical one.

3. **[KC-3] Google Search & other revenues decline in absolute terms for two consecutive quarters.** Search is the cash engine funding everything else. A sustained absolute revenue decline would mean AI-powered answers (from Google's own AI Overviews or competitors) are fundamentally disrupting the click-to-ad monetization model. This is the existential risk -- if Search revenue shrinks, the entire capital allocation framework collapses.
