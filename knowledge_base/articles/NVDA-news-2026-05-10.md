## Article 1: NVIDIA Q4 FY2026 Earnings Results

**Source:** NVIDIA News (nvidianews.nvidia.com)
**Date:** February 25, 2026
**Headline:** NVIDIA Announces Record Q4 and Full-Year FY2026 Results

### Key Takeaways

- Q4 FY2026 revenue reached $68.1B, up 73% year-over-year, beating the company's own guidance of ~$68B and marking another record quarter.
- Data Center revenue hit $62.3B in Q4 (+75% YoY), now representing 91.5% of total revenue, driven by insatiable demand for Blackwell and Hopper AI training/inference platforms.
- Full-year FY2026 revenue totaled $215.9B (+65% YoY), with net income of $120.1B and diluted EPS of $4.90.
- NVIDIA guided Q1 FY2027 revenue to approximately $78.0B, implying continued acceleration as Blackwell Ultra shipments ramp.
- The Rubin next-generation platform was officially unveiled, targeting H2 FY2027 launch to succeed Blackwell Ultra with significant performance-per-watt improvements.

### Relevance to Investment Thesis

NVIDIA's Q4 results demonstrate that AI infrastructure spending remains robust despite concerns about a potential spending slowdown. The $78B Q1 FY2027 guide (implying ~37% sequential growth from Q4's $68.1B on an annualized basis) signals continued hyperscaler demand visibility. The Rubin platform announcement provides a credible product roadmap extending into FY2028, addressing investor concerns about sustainability of the upgrade cycle. However, gross margin compression to 71.1% for the full year (driven by the $4.5B H20 charge and Blackwell transition costs) warrants monitoring.

## Article 2: Wall Street Skepticism Meets NVIDIA's AI Dominance

**Source:** CNBC
**Date:** February 24, 2026
**Headline:** Nvidia earnings collide with Wall Street skepticism over AI spending

### Key Takeaways

- NVIDIA was the only megacap technology stock with positive year-to-date returns entering Q4 earnings, reflecting continued investor confidence in AI infrastructure demand.
- Combined hyperscaler capital expenditure for 2026 is estimated at ~$700B, with the majority allocated to AI compute infrastructure where NVIDIA holds dominant market share.
- The Groq acquisition ($17B license deal) positions NVIDIA more aggressively in AI inference, addressing the competitive threat from custom inference chips and Groq's LPU (Language Processing Unit) architecture.
- Over 90% of Wall Street analysts maintained a Buy rating on NVIDIA heading into earnings, with consensus price targets implying significant upside from pre-earnings levels.
- Key risk highlighted: If AI revenue monetization disappoints at the hyperscaler level, capex spending could decelerate sharply, creating a demand cliff for NVIDIA's Data Center products.

### Relevance to Investment Thesis

This article frames the central bull/bear debate for NVIDIA. The bull case rests on sustained hyperscaler spending and NVIDIA's unchallenged position in AI training hardware, while the bear case centers on whether AI applications can generate sufficient ROI to justify $700B+ in annual capex. The Groq acquisition is a strategic response to the inference market becoming competitive. The near-unanimous analyst bullishness is worth noting as a potential contrarian signal. Any slowdown in hyperscaler AI spending guidance would be a material negative catalyst.

Any updated commentary on Rubin's competitive positioning versus AMD MI400 and custom ASICs will shape medium-term growth expectations. China export control developments remain the most significant exogenous risk factor.
