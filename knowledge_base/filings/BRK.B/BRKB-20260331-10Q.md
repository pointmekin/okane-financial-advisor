## Notable Changes in Investment Portfolio

### Equity Securities Activity

- **Net Selling:** Berkshire was a net seller of approximately $8.2 billion in equities during Q1 2026, marking the 14th consecutive quarter of net equity sales
- **Total Equity Portfolio Value:** Declined from $297.778B to $288.034B (-3.3%), driven by both net selling and mark-to-market losses
- **Top 5 Holdings:** American Express, Apple, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, Chevron — collectively representing ~61% of the equity portfolio

### Key Equity Method Investments

| Investment | Carrying Value | Fair Value | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kraft Heinz (27.5%) | $8.689B | $7.324B | Carrying exceeds FV by $1.4B (15.7%) |
| Occidental Petroleum (26.9%) | $10.808B | $17.221B | FV exceeds carrying by $6.4B |

**OxyChem Acquisition:** Completed January 2, 2026 for approximately $9.5 billion. This acquisition represents Berkshire's largest deal in recent years and deepens its exposure to the chemicals/energy sector through Occidental.

### Fixed Income

- Heavy allocation to U.S. Treasury Bills continued, with the T-bill portfolio growing from ~$290B to $339.3B
- Interest income from the cash/T-bill portfolio remains a significant contributor to operating earnings

### Other Notable Transactions

- **NICO Reinsurance Agreement:** National Indemnity Company entered into a whole account reinsurance agreement with Tokio Marine, with a 10-year term effective April 1, 2026. This is a significant reinsurance transaction that will generate premium volume over the coming decade.

## Guidance / Outlook

### Management Commentary

- **Warren Buffett** characterized the current investing environment as "not ideal," explaining why Berkshire continues to accumulate cash rather than deploy it into equities or acquisitions
- **Greg Abel**, in his first quarter as CEO, emphasized an "operational excellence" approach, including technology transformation and narrow AI adoption within operating businesses
- Abel stated Berkshire will not break up its conglomerate structure, reinforcing the long-standing strategy of owning diversified businesses under one roof
- Buffett gave Abel a "100% score" for his first quarter performance

### Segment Outlook

| Segment | Outlook |
|---|---|
| Insurance | GEICO faces competitive pressure; underwriting profitability may remain volatile. Tokio Marine reinsurance deal provides multi-year premium visibility. |
| BNSF | Continued volume growth expected; management focused on improving operating ratio (currently ranked 4th of 6 major railroads in margins). |
| BHE | PacifiCorp wildfire litigation ongoing — Oregon Court of Appeals reversed Phase I James verdict in April 2026, but future outcomes uncertain. Natural gas pipelines a bright spot. |
| Manufacturing | Expected to benefit from steady industrial demand. |
| Cash Deployment | No large acquisitions on the horizon; management continues to wait for attractive opportunities at the right price. |

### Capital Expenditure Plans

- **Q1 2026 Capex:** $4.986 billion
- **BNSF + BHE Full-Year Forecast:** ~$12.4 billion for remainder of 2026
- Continued heavy investment in railroad infrastructure and utility/regulatory compliance

### Share Repurchases

- **Q1 2026:** $235 million (minimal)
- Management continues to view shares as near fair value, limiting buyback activity
- Any significant increase in repurchases would signal management believes shares are undervalued

### Key Risks to Monitor

1. PacifiCorp wildfire litigation outcomes
2. GEICO competitive position in auto insurance
3. Interest rate environment (impacts T-bill income and investment portfolio valuations)
4. Macro economic slowdown affecting BNSF volumes and manufacturing demand
5. Execution risk under new CEO Greg Abel
