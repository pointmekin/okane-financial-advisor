## Business Overview

Berkshire Hathaway is a diversified holding company engaged in a broad range of businesses through its subsidiaries. The company operates through six reportable segments:

1. **Insurance** — Includes GEICO (auto insurance), Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group (commercial lines), and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group (BHRG). Insurance operations generate premium income and investment returns on "float" — funds held but not yet paid out in claims. As of year-end 2025, insurance float totaled approximately $176.9 billion.

2. **BNSF (Burlington Northern Santa Fe)** — One of the largest freight railroad networks in North America, operating approximately 32,500 route miles in 28 states and three Canadian provinces. Transports consumer products, industrial goods, coal, and agricultural products.

3. **Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE)** — A diversified energy holding company engaged in regulated electric and gas utilities, natural gas pipelines, and renewable energy generation. Major subsidiaries include MidAmerican Energy, PacifiCorp, and Northern PowerGrid.

4. **Manufacturing** — Includes precision metal parts (Precision Castparts), industrial products (Marmon, Lubrizol), building products (Clayton Homes, Shaw Industries, MiTek), and aerospace components.

5. **Service and Retailing** — Includes wholesale distribution (McLane), travel centers (Pilot Travel Centers), and retail operations (See's Candies, Nebraska Furniture Mart, Borsheims, Helzberg Diamonds).

6. **Finance and Financial Products** — Includes trailer leasing (XTRA), furniture leasing (CORT), and Clayton Homes financing operations.

**Leadership Transition:** On January 1, 2026, Greg Abel succeeded Warren Buffett as CEO of Berkshire Hathaway. Buffett remains Chairman of the Board. This marks the most significant leadership change in the company's modern history.

## Risk Factors (Top 5)

1. **Catastrophe and Insurance Risk** — Berkshire's insurance subsidiaries are exposed to significant catastrophe losses (hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires). A single major catastrophe event could generate billions in claims. Q4 2025 underwriting results were materially impacted by catastrophe losses.

2. **PacifiCorp Wildfire Liabilities** — BHE subsidiary PacifiCorp faces substantial wildfire-related litigation. Cumulative liabilities accrued totaled approximately $2.9 billion as of Q1 2026, with $2.3 billion paid to date. The Oregon Court of Appeals reversed the Phase I James verdict in April 2026, providing some potential relief, but future litigation outcomes remain uncertain.

3. **Concentration Risk in Investment Portfolio** — Approximately 61% of Berkshire's equity securities portfolio is concentrated in five holdings (American Express, Apple, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, Chevron). A significant decline in any single position could materially impact reported net earnings and book value.

4. **Leadership Transition Risk** — The transition from Warren Buffett to Greg Abel as CEO on January 1, 2026 introduces execution risk. While Abel has led Berkshire's non-insurance operations for years, the market may react to any perceived strategic shifts or if operational performance diverges from historical norms.

5. **Regulatory and Rate Risk** — BHE's regulated utilities are subject to rate case outcomes, environmental regulations, and renewable energy mandates. Adverse regulatory decisions or delays in cost recovery could pressure earnings in the energy segment.

## Share Repurchases

Berkshire resumed share repurchases in 2018 but activity has been minimal in recent quarters:

- **Q1 2026:** $235 million in share repurchases (minimal)
- **FY 2025:** Repurchases remained subdued, reflecting management's view that shares are not significantly undervalued
- **Policy:** Berkshire repurchases shares when the price is below management's estimate of intrinsic value, as determined by Buffett (and now Abel)

The low level of repurchases suggests management believes the current stock price is at or near fair value, and that holding cash for potential large acquisitions is a more attractive use of capital.

## Key Takeaways for Investors

1. **Fortress Balance Sheet:** ~$397B in cash and T-Bills provides unmatched financial strength and optionality
2. **Leadership Transition:** Greg Abel's first quarter (Q1 2026) showed operating earnings of $11.35B (+18% YoY), an encouraging start
3. **Insurance Float Machine:** $176.9B in float generates massive investment income at near-zero cost
4. **Valuation Signal:** Minimal share repurchases and continued net equity selling suggest management views both public markets and BRK's own stock as fully valued
5. **Wildfire Overhang:** PacifiCorp liabilities remain a wildcard; the Oregon Court of Appeals reversal is a positive but litigation continues
6. **Macro Hedge:** Massive cash position acts as a hedge against market dislocations — Berkshire is positioned to be a buyer of choice during the next crisis
